How Advantage Loan Tracker works

A free tracker for home loans linked to a savings account: see how much interest you pay each day, how much your savings balance saves you, when the loan will close, and whether your bank's figures match.

1. Who it is for

Borrowers with a savings-linked (overdraft-style) home loan, where the bank charges interest only on the outstanding loan minus the balance in a linked savings or current account. Banks sell these under names such as Home Loan Advantage, MaxGain or Money Saver. Every rupee you keep in the linked account cuts the interest for as long as it stays there, and you can still withdraw it.

It also works for an ordinary home loan: just leave the savings balance at zero.

2. What it tells you

  • How much interest is building up today, and what the next EMI will be made of (interest vs principal).
  • How much interest your savings balance has saved so far, and will save.
  • When the loan will close, and how that moves if you keep more in savings, prepay, or the rate changes.
  • Whether the bank's figures match: the EMI that left your savings reached the loan, and the interest and outstanding agree to the rupee.
  • Your yearly tax picture (Sec 24(b) interest, 80C principal) and where your money goes each month.

3. How the interest is worked out

It follows the scheme rules banks print in the sanction letter:

Interest for a day = max(0, outstanding − savings balance at end of day) × rate ÷ 365

Example: ₹32,00,000 outstanding, ₹0 in savings, 7.30% a year → 32,00,000 × 7.30% ÷ 365 = ₹640 a day. Keep ₹5,00,000 in the linked account and interest is charged on ₹27,00,000 only → ₹540 a day, saving ₹100 every day.

  • Monthly rests: the day-by-day interest is added up and charged with each EMI.
  • Only money paid out carries interest. If the loan is released in parts (disbursements), each part counts from the day it is paid; future parts can be added as "planned".
  • EMI stays fixed. When the rate changes, the tenure moves instead, as banks usually do. Enter the date the bank applies the new rate.
  • The future is projected from your salary and expense plan (Smart payoff), with the EMI taken out on each EMI date.
  • Bank figures win. Once your loan statement shows an EMI posted, that month uses the bank's own interest and outstanding, so the tracker and the bank stay identical and projections start from the real balance.

4. Getting started (about 10 minutes)

  1. Sign in with Google and accept the terms once.
  2. Add a loan: a name, the bank, the sanctioned amount, the rate, the tenure in months, the disbursal date and the first EMI date (all on your sanction letter). You can add more than one loan.
  3. Savings balance: enter today's balance on the Overview, add past balances under Savings balance (one entry each time it changed), paste many lines at once, or upload the savings statement.
  4. Loan statement: upload it under Savings balance → Upload bank statements together with the savings one, or enter each month's interest under History.
  5. Check History → Bank match: every posted EMI should read ✓ Matched.

Optional: set your salary and expenses in Smart payoff for a payoff plan, and your tax slab in Tax.

6. Sharing and who can see what

Each loan is private. You, the owner, decide who else sees it, from Loan settings → Sharing:

  • Invite by email: type their Google email, choose Viewer or Editor, tap Invite. They get access at once and find the loan under Shared with me when they sign in. Tap Copy link and send it to them on Gmail, WhatsApp or anything else.
  • Or send the link: Copy link, send it; they sign in and tap Request access; you approve or decline.

7. Backups, salary password, deleting

  • Daily backups are made in the cloud when you open your loan; Back up now makes one any time. View any backup to see what it holds and what changed, and restore it. You can also download a backup file.
  • Salary password (Smart payoff): Show salary then asks for it, and the salary hides again after 5 idle minutes. Setting or changing it needs a fresh Google sign-in.
  • Delete a loan from the account menu (your photo) or at the end of Loan settings: type the loan's name, confirm with Google, and the loan, its data and its backups are deleted for good.

8. Monthly routine (5 minutes)

  1. Download the savings and home loan statements for the same period from your bank's official site or app.
  2. Savings balance → Upload bank statements: choose both → Read files → Import.
  3. Check History → Bank match.

9. Good to know

  • Statements from any bank can be read: Excel and CSV files are read by their column names (date, debit/withdrawal, credit/deposit or amount with Dr/Cr, balance), and every row is checked to add up. If a file's columns aren't recognised, or the balances don't add up, you choose the columns once and the layout is remembered. PDFs are read from their text; if one comes out wrong, download the same statement as Excel or CSV. Scanned (image) PDFs can't be read.
  • Figures after today are estimates. For tax filing, use your bank's interest certificate.
  • The app works on phones and can be installed (account menu → Install app); it keeps an offline copy.
Stay safe: this app never asks for your net-banking password, OTP, card details or UPI PIN, and it never connects to your bank. See Terms & disclaimer.

10. Questions

Is my data safe?

It is stored in Google Firebase (Mumbai region) behind Google sign-in and security rules that allow only you, the people you approve, and the app's administrator (read-only) to read it. Statement files are read in your browser; only the figures are saved.

Does it connect to my bank or move money?

No. It never logs in to your bank and cannot make payments. It only uses what you type or the statement files you pick.

Why is it a few rupees off from my bank?

Banks round differently and post some charges on their own dates. Once a loan statement month is imported, the tracker uses the bank's own figures for that month.

Can I track more than one loan?

Yes. Add as many as you like from My loans; each has its own data and sharing list.

Can I delete everything?

Yes, per loan: Delete this loan removes the loan, all its data and its backups for good.

Is it financial advice?

No. It is a calculator for your own figures. Decide with your bank's statements and, if needed, a professional.
