Home loan tax benefits: Section 24(b) and 80C

A home loan can lower your income tax in two ways: a deduction for the interest you pay and one for the principal you repay. Which ones you get depends on whether the home is self-occupied or let out, and on your tax regime.

Interest: Section 24(b)

  • Self-occupied home (old regime): interest paid in the year can be deducted up to ₹2,00,000.
  • Construction must be finished within five years from the end of the financial year in which the loan was taken for the full ₹2,00,000 limit; otherwise the limit for a self-occupied home drops to ₹30,000.
  • Interest paid before possession (pre-construction interest) is deducted in five equal parts, starting the year construction is completed, within the same yearly limit.
  • Let-out home: the whole interest is deductible against the rent, but a resulting loss can be set off against other income only up to ₹2,00,000 a year; the rest is carried forward.

Principal: Section 80C

  • The principal part of your EMIs counts towards Section 80C, which has an overall limit of ₹1,50,000 a year shared with PPF, EPF, life insurance, ELSS and the rest.
  • Stamp duty and registration charges can also be claimed under 80C, in the year you pay them.
  • If you sell the house within five years of the end of the year you got possession, the 80C deductions claimed are added back to your income in the year of sale.

Old regime vs new regime

Old regimeNew regime
Interest on a self-occupied home (24(b))Up to ₹2,00,000Not allowed
Interest on a let-out homeAgainst rent; loss set-off up to ₹2,00,000Against rent only; a loss can't be set off against other income
Principal (80C)Up to ₹1,50,000 (shared limit)Not allowed

The new regime is the default unless you choose the old one. Whether the home-loan deductions make the old regime better depends on your other deductions and income: compare both each year.

Joint home loans

If the home is jointly owned and the loan is jointly taken, each co-owner who pays the EMI can claim their own deductions, each with their own limits. That can double the benefit for a couple. Keep the ownership share and the EMI payments consistent with what you claim.

Savings-linked loans and tax

A balance in your linked account lowers the interest you pay, and so lowers what you can claim under 24(b). That is fine: you never save more tax than the interest you pay. If your yearly interest is above ₹2,00,000, the part you save above the limit wasn't deductible anyway.

What to keep

  • The interest certificate from your bank for each financial year: it splits interest and principal. Use it for filing, not an estimate.
  • The possession or completion certificate, and stamp-duty and registration receipts.
