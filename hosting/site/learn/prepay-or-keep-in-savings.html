Prepay the loan or keep the money in savings?

You have a bonus or some spare savings. With an ordinary home loan, prepaying is the usual way to cut interest. With a savings-linked loan there is a second option: leave the money in the linked account. Here is how they compare.

They save the same interest

On a savings-linked loan, interest each day is charged on outstanding − linked balance. Paying ₹2,00,000 off the loan lowers the outstanding by ₹2,00,000; keeping ₹2,00,000 in the linked account lowers the amount interest is charged on by the same ₹2,00,000. As long as the money stays put, the interest saved is the same.

Example: ₹32,00,000 at 7.30%. Either prepaying ₹2,00,000 or keeping it in the linked account cuts the interest from ₹640 to ₹600 a day: about ₹14,600 in a year.

How to choose

  • Money you may need (emergency fund, school fees, a car next year): keep it in the linked account. You get the loan-rate saving and can still use it.
  • Money you are sure you won't need and might be tempted to spend: prepaying locks the saving in.
  • If the linked balance already covers your cushion, extra money can go either way: the interest result is the same.
  • Investing instead: compare the return you expect after tax with your loan rate. Keeping money in the linked account is like a safe, tax-free return equal to your loan rate.

Charges and rules

  • RBI does not allow banks to charge a prepayment or foreclosure penalty on floating-rate home loans to individual borrowers. Fixed-rate loans can carry a charge; check your sanction letter.
  • Some banks set a minimum amount for a part-prepayment, or ask you to request it at a branch or online.
  • After a prepayment, banks usually keep the EMI and shorten the tenure. If you would rather have a lower EMI, ask.

A tax point

Lower interest means a smaller deduction under Sec 24(b) (old tax regime, up to ₹2,00,000 a year for a self-occupied home). If your yearly interest is well above ₹2,00,000, saving some of it costs you no deduction at all; if it is below, part of the saving is offset by a smaller deduction. See home loan tax benefits.
