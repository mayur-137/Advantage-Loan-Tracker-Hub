How savings-linked home loans work

Some home loans come joined to a savings or current account. Money you keep in that account is treated as if it were paid off the loan, for as long as it stays there, so you pay less interest without giving up access to it.

The one rule that matters

A normal home loan charges interest on the full outstanding amount. A savings-linked loan charges interest only on the outstanding amount minus the balance in the linked account, worked out every day:

Interest for a day = (outstanding − linked balance at the end of the day) × annual rate ÷ 365

The daily amounts are added up and charged once a month, usually with your EMI (the bank calls this "monthly rests"). The balance can never push the interest below zero: if you keep more than the outstanding, the extra earns nothing.

A worked example

Take a loan of ₹32,00,000 at 7.30% a year.

₹0₹32,00,000₹640–
₹2,00,000₹30,00,000₹600₹14,600
₹5,00,000₹27,00,000₹540₹36,500
₹10,00,000₹22,00,000₹440₹73,000

Every ₹1 lakh kept in the account saves about ₹20 a day at this rate (1,00,000 × 7.30% ÷ 365).

Why the loan finishes early

Your EMI does not change. But the interest part of each EMI is smaller, so a bigger part goes to repaying the loan itself. Month after month that adds up: the outstanding falls faster and the last EMI comes sooner. On a long loan, keeping a few lakh in the account steadily can take years off the tenure. Two common designs

  • A separate linked savings account. Your loan account and a savings account are tied together; the savings balance is set against the loan each day. Bank of Baroda's Home Loan Advantage works like this.
  • The loan account itself works like an overdraft. You deposit spare money straight into the loan account and can withdraw it again, up to a limit. SBI's MaxGain is a well-known example.

The effect on interest is the same in both. The names, limits and fees differ, so read your sanction letter and the scheme's terms.

What it can cost

  • The interest rate is sometimes a little higher than the same bank's plain home loan, and some banks charge a fee or need a minimum balance.
  • The benefit only comes if you actually keep money there. If the balance is usually near zero, a cheaper plain loan may be better.
  • Lower interest also means a smaller interest deduction under Sec 24(b) if you claim it. See home loan tax benefits.

Who it suits

People who keep a cushion of savings anyway: an emergency fund, a bonus waiting to be used, money for a planned expense months away. Parked in the linked account, that money earns, in effect, your loan rate, tax-free, while staying available. Compare that with a savings account paying 2–3% (taxable) or a fixed deposit, whose interest is taxable too.
