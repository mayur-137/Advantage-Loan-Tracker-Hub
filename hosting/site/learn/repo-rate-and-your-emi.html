Repo rate changes and your EMI

Most new floating-rate home loans in India are linked to an external benchmark, usually RBI's repo rate. When the repo rate moves, your loan rate follows. Here is what actually changes, and what doesn't.

How the link works

Your loan rate is the benchmark plus a fixed margin (the "spread") set in your sanction letter. For a repo-linked loan (often called an EBLR or RLLR loan): loan rate = repo rate + spread. If the repo rate rises by 0.25%, so does your loan rate.

RBI requires the rate on these loans to be reset at least once every three months. Banks follow their own reset dates, so a change can reach your loan a few weeks after RBI announces it, often from the first day of the following month.

Why your EMI usually stays the same

When the rate goes up, most banks keep the EMI and lengthen the tenure; when it goes down, they keep the EMI and shorten the tenure. The EMI you see each month looks unchanged, but the number of EMIs left moves.

Example: ₹32,00,000 over 24 years at 7.30% has an EMI of about ₹23,600. If the rate rises to 7.80% and the EMI is kept the same, the loan runs noticeably longer: years, not months. Each 0.25% matters over a long loan.

What you can ask for

RBI's 2023 guidelines on resetting floating rates ask banks to explain the impact of a rate change clearly and to give borrowers choices at a reset, such as:

  • a higher EMI instead of a longer tenure (or a mix of both);
  • prepaying part or all of the loan at any point;
  • switching to a fixed rate, as the bank's policy allows.

Banks must also share a statement each quarter showing the principal and interest recovered so far, the EMI, the number of EMIs left and the annual rate. If you don't get it, ask.

Savings-linked loans and rate changes

A balance in your linked account protects you a little from rises: interest is charged only on the outstanding minus that balance, so a higher rate costs less on a loan with a healthy balance. Every ₹1 lakh in the account saves about ₹21 a day at 7.80% instead of ₹20 at 7.30%.

Keeping track

Enter each rate change with the date your bank applies it (Loan settings → Rate changes in the tracker). The schedule then shows the new payoff date, and the What-if tab can simulate a future repo change.
