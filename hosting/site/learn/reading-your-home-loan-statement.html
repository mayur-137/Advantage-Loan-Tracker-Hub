Reading your home loan statement

A home loan account statement looks like a bank statement turned upside down: the balance is what you owe, and it goes up with debits. Once you know the four kinds of entry, it is easy to check.

The balance is what you owe

The running balance on a loan statement is the outstanding: principal still to repay, plus any interest charged and not yet paid. Many banks print it with "Dr" after it, because the account is in debit. It rises when the bank pays out money or charges interest, and falls when your EMI comes in.

The four kinds of entry

During construction you may see only interest and small payments (pre-EMI interest) before full EMIs start.

Checking the interest yourself

  1. Take the outstanding on each day of the month. On a savings-linked loan, subtract that day's closing balance in the linked account (never below zero).
  2. Multiply by the annual rate and divide by 365. That is the day's interest.
  3. Add up the days from one interest posting to the next. The total should match the interest entry, give or take a rupee of rounding.

Example: ₹32,00,000 outstanding at 7.30% with nothing in savings is ₹640 a day, about ₹19,840 for a 31-day month.

Checking the EMI went through

The EMI should leave your savings account and reach the loan account for the same amount, around the EMI date. If the savings side shows the debit but the loan side shows less (or nothing), ask the bank: it may be sitting in a suspense account.

Where to get the statement

Download it from your bank's official website or app, usually as PDF or Excel. Excel or CSV is easier for tools to read than PDF; a scanned (image) PDF can't be read automatically at all. Some PDFs are password-protected, often with a combination of your name and date of birth, as your bank explains.
